## Active V cards

### V001 · specious · Box 2
Meaning: plausible or convincing on the surface but actually wrong · Use note: stronger than "false": it says the argument LOOKS right. Used of arguments, reasoning, claims. · Confusable with: spurious (not genuine; a fake or false link), fallacious
Example (from lesson): "Attended, so serious" (affirming the consequent) is a specious argument.

### V002 · preclude · Box 2
Meaning: to rule something out in advance; to make it impossible or prevent it · Use note: "A does not preclude B" = B can still happen. Takes an event or possibility as its object (preclude a settlement), not an existing thing. · Confusable with: prevent, obviate (removes the NEED for a step), vitiate (spoils something that exists)
Example (from lesson): A missed opening session precludes being serious, because attending is necessary.

History: ✔ produce Lesson #8; loose in Lesson #13; ✘ Lesson #14 GV1 (picked *preclude* for *vitiate*); ✔ cloze Lesson #15 (Box 2).

### V003 · tenuous · Box 2
Meaning: very weak or thin; barely supported · Use note: of links, connections, arguments; the support is slight, not that the claim is false. · Confusable with: tenable (defensible), tentative (provisional)
Example (from lesson): "Most speak French. Some speak Arabic. So someone speaks both" is a tenuous inference.

### V004 · ubiquitous · Box 2
Meaning: present or found everywhere · Use note: describes how widespread something is, not a logical "all". · Confusable with: universal, omnipresent
Example (from lesson): "Smartphones are ubiquitous in the capital, but 'every resident owns one' would need a separate claim."

### V005 · obviate · Box 3
Meaning: to make something unnecessary; to remove the need for it · Use note: takes a need or step as its object ("X obviates the need for Y"). · Confusable with: obliterate, avoid, preclude
Example (from lesson): A rule for finding the real opposite of a claim obviates the need to guess which statements "feel" opposite.

### V006 · repudiate · Box 2
Meaning: to reject as invalid, untrue or unacceptable; to refuse to accept or be bound by (a claim, agreement, debt, one's own earlier statement) · Use note: stronger than "deny"; it rejects a claim's authority. · Confusable with: refute, deny, recant
Example (from lesson): The new government repudiated the debt, but economists could not refute the old government's accounts.

### V007 · ineluctable · Box 2
Meaning: impossible to avoid or struggle free from; unavoidable · Use note: more formal and fatalistic than "inevitable". · Confusable with: inevitable, ineffable (inexpressible), inescapable
Example (from lesson): Athens treats the rule of the strong over the weak as ineluctable.

### V008 · bellicose · Box 3
Meaning: eager to fight; inclined to aggression or war · Use note: describes a disposition or stance, not a single act. · Confusable with: belligerent (actually hostile or at war), pugnacious
Example (from lesson): A bellicose leader gives speeches about war; a belligerent state has already crossed the border.

### V009 · paucity · Box 2

### V010 · plethora · Box 3
Meaning: a large excess; more than is needed or wanted · Use note: carries a sense of too much, not just "many". · Confusable with: abundance, glut
Example (from lesson): At $5 sellers offer 80 sacks and buyers want only 40: a plethora of coffee at that price.

### V011 · precipitate · Box 2
Meaning: (verb) to trigger or bring on something suddenly or sooner than expected · Use note: takes an event as its object; the adjective (hasty) and noun (a chemical solid) are different words. · Confusable with: cause, hasten
Example (from lesson): The frost precipitated a sharp rise in coffee prices.

### V012 · spurious · Box 2
Meaning: not genuine or authentic; having no real basis (a false or illusory link, e.g. a spurious correlation) · Use note: says the thing is not real; *specious* says it LOOKS convincing but is wrong. · Confusable with: specious, fallacious, sporadic (occasional)
Example (from lesson): Comparing a ratio with a raw count gives a spurious comparison.

### V013 · prodigious · Box 2
Meaning: remarkably great in size, amount or extent · Use note: of quantities, efforts, appetites, talents; not *prodigal* (wasteful) or *prodigy*. · Confusable with: prodigal, prodigy, voluminous, copious
Example (from lesson): A prodigious backlog of visa cases built up while the processing center was understaffed.

### V014 · vitiate · Box 2
Meaning: to spoil or weaken something so that it loses its force or validity · Use note: of arguments, contracts, results; a single flaw can vitiate the whole. · Confusable with: preclude (rules out in advance), obviate (removes the need), invalidate
Example (from lesson): A single forged signature can vitiate an otherwise valid contract; a late filing vitiates the whole application.

### V015 · tacit · Box 2
Meaning: understood or implied without being stated · Use note: "a tacit assumption" is one the author never states; do not say "tacitly implied" or "tacitly understood" (redundant). · Confusable with: implicit (close), taciturn (a person who says little)
Example (from lesson): The treaty never states the obligation outright, but it is tacit in every clause.

### V016 · exigent · Box 2
Meaning: pressing; demanding immediate attention or action · Use note: of needs, demands, circumstances ("exigent circumstances"); stronger than "urgent" in formal use. · Confusable with: exacting (very demanding), urgent, ineluctable (unavoidable)
Example (from lesson): Gasoline is an exigent need for a commuter, which is why demand for it is inelastic.

### V017 · palliate · Box 2
Meaning: to ease the symptoms of something without curing its cause · Use note: takes a problem or a pain as its object; not "placate" (to calm a person). · Confusable with: placate, mollify, alleviate, mitigate
Example (from lesson): A gas-tax holiday palliates the pain at the pump, but it does not add a barrel of oil.

### V018 · perfidious · Box 2
Meaning: deliberately betraying trust; treacherous · Use note: of people or acts of betrayal (the noun is *perfidy*), not of something merely unreliable; formal and strong. · Confusable with: perfunctory (done without care), pernicious (harmful), treacherous
Example (from lesson): With the ring of Gyges, the shepherd is perfidious: he seduces the queen and murders the king, because no one can see him.

### V019 · sagacious · Box 1
Meaning: having or showing shrewd, sound judgment; wise in practical matters · Use note: praise for judgment (a sagacious adviser); the noun is *sagacity*; a touch more practical than "wise". · Confusable with: salacious (lewd), sapient (wise, more intellectual), sage
Example (from lesson): Plato presents Socrates as the sagacious figure who sees that the man ruled by his appetites is not free.

### V020 · ostensible · Box 2
Meaning: apparent or stated, but possibly not the real reason or truth · Use note: usually describes a stated purpose or cause that may hide the real one ("the ostensible reason"); adverb *ostensibly*. · Confusable with: ostentatious (showy), apparent
Example (from lesson): The ostensible cause of the sales rise was the car ban; the 12% price increase may be the real one.

### V021 · trenchant · Box 2
Meaning: forceful, clear and incisive (of a criticism, argument or analysis) · Use note: praise for sharp, penetrating commentary ("a trenchant objection exposes the gap"); a person's wit can be trenchant too. · Confusable with: strident (harsh in tone), acerbic, pellucid
Example (from lesson): A trenchant objection exposes the gap between "who pays" and "who benefits".

### V022 · equivocate · Box 1
Meaning: to use vague or double-meaning language to avoid committing to a position; as a fallacy (*equivocation*), a word quietly changes meaning mid-argument · Use note: a verb; the adjective *equivocal* means ambiguous or noncommittal; not "equivocal" for "equal". · Confusable with: prevaricate (dodge the truth), vacillate (swing between positions), unequivocal (the opposite: clear)
Example (from lesson): Asked "will you raise taxes?", a politician who answers "I'll protect the middle class" is equivocating.

### V023 · fatuous · Box 1
Meaning: silly and pointless, often with a smug or complacent air · Use note: of remarks, ideas, arguments (a fatuous bridge restates the premise); the noun is *fatuity*. · Confusable with: facetious (joking), futile (useless), vacuous
Example (from lesson): A "bridge" that just restates the premise ("strong bridges are strong") is fatuous.
